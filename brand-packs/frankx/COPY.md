# FrankX Editorial Language

FrankX copy should sound like one alert person speaking to another. The writing
earns attention through observation, judgment, and useful evidence.

## Reader and decision

Write for founders, creators, AI builders, and ambitious professionals choosing tools, systems, offers, and business models. The reader should leave with Frank's actual view and enough evidence to make a decision.

## Order of Truth

For a high-value page, the first 120 words should establish:

1. why this page exists for this reader;
2. what Frank actually saw, made, tested, or learned;
3. the smallest honest proposition for a next move.

Mechanisms come after the human reason. “Agent,” “skill,” “graph,” “system,” and
“infrastructure” are useful only when they make the proof more precise.

## Sentence Standard

- Prefer a concrete noun and active verb.
- Vary sentence length; let the important sentence stand alone.
- Make one claim per sentence.
- Put the proof next to the claim.
- Use the recipient's name only when the page contains authorized, specific context.
- Write calls to action as human choices: reply, compare, try, inspect, meet.
- Read the copy aloud. Rewrite any line Frank would not naturally say.
- Use first person when the judgment comes from Frank's real work.
- Name prices, trade-offs, experiments, failures, and current decisions when known.

## Required Tests

### Name-swap test

Replace the person or product name. If the paragraph still works unchanged, it is
generic and cannot ship.

### Builder-taxonomy test

Remove words such as AI, agent, skill, infrastructure, ecosystem, sovereign,
substrate, constellation, and authority graph. If the meaning disappears, the
sentence was hiding behind taxonomy.

### Concrete-anchor test

Every important claim needs one observable anchor: a moment, artifact, decision,
result, quotation, or working behavior.

### Deletion test

Delete the grandest adjective and the most abstract noun. If the line improves,
keep the deletion.

## Avoid

- “The infrastructure already exists.”
- “A sovereign agent fleet.”
- “This is not presentationware.”
- Rhetorical contrast formulas built as “X, not Y.”
- “Tool intelligence,” “operating-role routes,” and other invented labels that hide a simple action.
- “Unlock,” “reimagine,” “elevate,” or “transform” without a measurable object.
- False intimacy, invented quotations, synthetic praise, or implied endorsement.
- Explaining Frank's entire ecosystem before earning the reader's interest.
- Consultant language, omniscient claims, slogan fragments, and performative cleverness.

## Example

Weak:

> Transform your future with a next-generation intelligence substrate.

Specific:

> You turned a live audience question into a usable method in under three minutes.
> I built a small working kit around that move. If it feels true to your process,
> I would like to test it with you for one week.

## Evidence-led founder products and the research-to-value narrative — proposal, 4 Nov 2026

Preserve the [canonical human Voice OS](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1nVOppnlEiTOlutyA7Y5JufIyDWBKjUNqNT8wmJYvb1M/edit): recognize the person, state one consequential thesis, show the mechanism and real work, then narrow the next decision. The approved Google Doc governs FrankX personal voice before any skill or Notion projection; this candidate cannot supersede it.

FrankX's lived work is the story: original research, AI architecture, building creator tools, music, family and human relationships. Technical ideas become credible through exact examples and what another builder could use or adapt, not exaggerated superiority.

- Candidate opening: "I build the systems I want to own, then share what holds up in real use."
- Candidate proof line: "We tested one workflow against a real project. Here's the edited artifact, what it cost, and what still fails."
- Advisory invitation after verified delivery: "Bring one difficult architecture decision. Leave with the tradeoffs and a working plan."

A meaningful invitation to a founder circle or toolkit names what members actually get, what is not included, the cancellation/support boundary and the real skill or result involved. Avoid false scarcity, elite prestige theater, "passive income guaranteed" and pretending an AI role is a human colleague. One editorial research result can become an article, a short video, a workbook and a tested customer artifact—but publication/rights and deliverability remain distinct gates.
