# Material direction and critique prompts

Status: candidate recipes. Prompts create proposals; inspected outputs and source
records determine acceptance. Do not imply that these prompts have been run.

## Code or Figma implementation

> Extend the existing Starlight lab brand workbench using the current owned
> identity manifest and unchanged master vectors. Read the selected product's
> instructions, the SIS source authority, foundations README and tokens.json.
> Keep the product's actual type, navigation, spacing and downloads. Build one
> large Starlight material specimen with Obsidian, Porcelain and Liquid glass
> controls and base/raised/floating depth. Put instructions and controls on
> readable opaque surfaces. Use a top-left warm key light, a narrow cool glass
> rim and stable contact shadow. Limit static grain to 0.025 opacity within
> the specimen, blur to 18px and visible non-nested backdrop surfaces to two.
> A user-triggered 1800ms sequence resolves no more than 24 points on desktop
> or 12 below 768px, then rests. Reduced motion shows the final state directly.
> Keep mark geometry, the center and companion dot. Material is an application,
> not a replacement logo. Report keyboard behavior, reflow, real fonts,
> fallback, initial/active/rest frames and exact source revision. Keep unsupported
> runtime or release claims out of the interface.

## Material exploration image

Use inspected, rights-cleared references. An image generator may produce an
application study, never the master vector. Keep overlays and essential text
outside the generated image.

> Produce a candidate Starlight material study using the supplied owned star
> reference as the subject. Preserve its four-point silhouette and companion
> relationship. A single object rests in a quiet architectural recess; show
> clear optical thickness and a narrow cyan edge in the glass treatment,
> broad soft reflections and mass in porcelain, or restrained warm reflections
> in obsidian. One light source enters from upper left; the contact shadow
> grounds the object. The frame is composed for a material specimen with useful
> negative space, visible edge detail and a clean silhouette at thumbnail size.
> No invented letters, interface chrome, slogans, new insignia, characters or
> all-over rainbow reflection. Show one material per image. Deliver a candidate
> subject study; retain the supplied vector as the source of identity truth.

Inspect each result at full size and thumbnail size. Reject melted star tips,
an absent companion, fused edges, impossible material transitions, accidental
glyphs, halos hiding the silhouette, stock space imagery and excessive microdetail.
Keep a rejected study as rejected; do not call a generation canon or production.

## Motion graphic brief

> Create an authored sequence from the accepted vector/material study, using
> the selected material's existing light direction. Start with a readable still
> composition. On the named trigger, a bounded set of points resolves around the
> unchanged object, then rests by 1800ms. The object anchor and caption remain
> stable. The sequence communicates spatial relation and brand memory, not a
> claim of agent execution. Produce distinct 16:9 and 9:16 layouts, a static
> final poster and a reduced-motion equivalent. Keep captions as editable text.
> Deliver decoded ordered frames with viewport, DPR, time, dimensions and hashes;
> inspect the beginning, active motion, settling frame and static hold. Do not
> accept a video filename as evidence that the export was checked.

## Independent critique

> Review the actual rendered Starlight workbench without the maker's desired
> verdict. Identify the user's next action in five seconds, then select every
> material and depth with pointer and keyboard. Judge whether each material has
> a distinct physical reading while the form stays recognizable. Test 375px,
> 768px and 1440px widths, 200% zoom, reduced motion, opaque fallback and forced
> colors. Check contrast on actual composited backgrounds, real font loading,
> focus, interruption, hidden-tab behavior and stable final frames. Challenge
> any implication that animation proves activity or success. Record defects by
> impact, the exact revision and evidence. Reject a visually attractive result
> if its controls, reading, identity or state truth fail. Do not inflate a
> browser-only pass into production or customer acceptance.
