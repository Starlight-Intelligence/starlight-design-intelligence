# Lineages and fusion

The supplied artwork establishes four coherent territories. This release names candidate characters and professional roles within those territories. It does not approve new brand identities or change an existing character's canon.

## The four families

| Family | Form and material grammar | Environment | Product use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Porcelain Observatory | Compact porcelain figures, ink joints, restrained brass/cyan, readable tools | Archive desks, observatory worktables, paper artifacts | Core mission workspace, maker/reviewer roles, first-minute explanation |
| Noble Machines | Upright ceremonial humanoid or animal silhouettes, ivory ceramic, sapphire enamel, fine gold seams | Sunlit limestone conservatory, generous architectural space | Stewardship, architecture, quality review, enterprise invitation |
| Prismatic Tide | Aquatic forms, pearl faces, translucent fins/shells, refraction, precise brass articulation | Ocean observatory, dark basalt, dusk and reflected water | Research maps, onboarding, archives, flow and provenance |
| Living Glasshouse | Botanical and woodland forms, speckled clay, timber, cork, linen, moss, contained terrarium glass | Working greenhouse, natural light, seed trays, quiet desks | Knowledge gardens, learning, care, curation, steady maintenance |

Noble means poise, precision, and service. It does not require a crown, rank inflation, or an all-powerful personality. Glass communicates light and internal structure; it does not justify translucent text panels. Natural elements should reveal the profession through a useful prop, posture, or interaction.

## Design-parent model

Record ancestry on distinct axes:

- **Form parent:** humanoid, manta, heron, ram, axolotl, nautilus, tortoise, mushroom, or botanical figure.
- **Material parent:** porcelain, cast glass, enamel, brass, timber, cork, linen, or clay.
- **Ecological parent:** ocean, greenhouse, archive, or observatory setting.
- **Interaction parent:** orient, compare, map, inspect, collect, assemble, or recover.

A character usually has one primary family and one clearly bounded secondary influence. Record a crossover as a new candidate edition with explicit overrides. Keep the source character identity intact. Do not resolve conflicting visual parents by concatenating every feature.

## Fusion recipes

| Recipe | Keep | Introduce | Reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Noble × Tide | Noble stance and restrained seam work | One translucent fin, horn, or core; water-inspired light | Every surface becoming glass; unreadable silhouette |
| Glasshouse × Observatory | A functional task and porcelain face grammar | A small contained botanical mechanism or timber tool | Unbounded foliage hiding eyes, hands, or controls |
| Tide × Observatory | Aquatic form and small face anchor | A precise archive/map instrument; paper or chart staging | Floating random UI cards inside the image |
| Noble × Glasshouse | Deliberate animal/humanoid structure | Matte mineral surfaces and one living material motif | Costume clutter or implied ecological claims |

Use a silhouette budget: one dominant contour, one secondary gesture, one signature feature. At 32px, simplify texture and articulation; at 160px, reveal profession and material; in a scene, reveal environment and collaboration. Texture intensity, palette, and motion may change by rendition. Identity anchors do not.

## Prompt grammar

Use this structure with actual inspected references:

> Create a candidate rendition of [character ID/name] for [user task and placement]. Preserve [three recognition anchors] from [source references]. Use [primary family] with [bounded secondary influence]. Show [one professional action] through [prop/posture]. Materials: [specific substrate, finish, seams, translucency]. Environment: [purposeful setting]. Light and camera: [controlled choices]. Frame for [size/aspect/crop]. Keep UI text, logos, factual diagrams, and partner marks out of the generated layer. Output remains a design candidate pending inspection.

For identity sheets, request neutral lighting and consistent views before a cinematic scene. For UI avatars, preserve clear space and a simple background or transparency. For motion, name the event the gesture represents and provide a static/reduced-motion equivalent. Never pass a descriptive moodboard off as an exact identity reference.

## Material-to-interface translation

Carry the family's authored material into a limited UI accent: the edge of an avatar frame, an illustration, a separator, or the reveal of a selected relationship. Put labels, controls, permissions, and evidence on readable surfaces. Keep success/error colors independent from a character's favorite color. Use semantic tokens, then map those tokens into the owning app's actual theme.

The desired recognition is “I know who does this work.” Validate it through role-matching and return-visit tasks before scaling the cast.
